01 — Get comfortable in water

Before you swim, get comfortable in the water.

Nobody starts with a stroke. You start by standing, entering and letting the water feel normal.

What you are learning right now

Water confidence — the first core skill.

02 — Breathing / bubbles

Above water — calm inhaleSTEP 01 / 04

Breathe with the water.

Beginners hold their breath. Swimmers breathe out slowly, the whole time their face is in.

The swimmer never disappears between steps — it moves from one position into the next.

04 — Body position & streamline

NORMAL — head lifted, hips low, more resistanceDRAG ●●●●○

Make your body one line.

Streamline — make your body long and narrow so you move through the water more easily.

Longer and more streamlined means less resistance.

  • HeadEyes down, in line with your spine.
  • ArmsStretched past your ears.
  • CoreGently tight so hips stay up.
  • Legs & toesLong and pointed, not bent.

05 — Push & glide

Push. Stretch. Glide.

Your first real travel through water comes from the wall, not from your arms.

06 — Kicking

Now add your legs.

A flutter kick is small, steady and comes from the hip — not from the knee.

  • 01Keep legs longThink of kicking with the whole leg.
  • 02Keep knees relaxedA small natural bend, never a bicycle.
  • 03Point your toesLoose ankles push more water.
  • 04Kick from the hipsThe movement starts high, not low.

    Water safety — not technique

    Swimming starts with being safe.

    This section is deliberately separated from the lesson content. It applies before, during and after every session.

    01Safe entryUse steps or the wall. Check the depth before you get in.
    02Safe exitKnow how you will get out before you start.
    03Know your depthStay where you can stand until floating feels easy.
    04Never swim aloneA lifeguard or a companion, every single time.
    05Follow pool rulesLane direction, diving zones and signage exist for a reason.
    06Know when to ask for helpStop, stand or signal early — not once you are tired.

    This guide explains movement. It is not a replacement for swimming lessons or a qualified instructor.

    Example beginner practice structure

    Your first session.

    Example structure only — not medical or professional advice.
    Adjust with a qualified instructor.

    5 minGet comfortable — stand, enter, relax
    5 minBubbles — face in, breathe out slowly
    5 minFloating — front, back, recover to standing
    5 minPush & glide — one line from the wall
    10 minKick — with a float, short repeats
    10 minFreestyle basics — glide, one arm, breathe
    5 minEasy swim / rest — finish calm
    40 minTotal — stop earlier if you feel tired
